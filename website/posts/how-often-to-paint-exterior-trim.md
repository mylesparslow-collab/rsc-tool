Exterior wood trim typically needs repainting every five to ten years, with the south and west sides, window sills, porch rails and other horizontal surfaces coming due sooner than the rest. The right time is when the paint starts to crack, peel or chalk, and before bare wood is exposed to a winter. Waiting until the wood itself is gray and soft turns a paint job into a carpentry job, which is the most common and avoidable expense we see on older houses.

## Why trim wears faster than siding

Trim is where water collects. Window sills face up and hold rain and melting snow. Corner boards, casings and fascia have joints and end grain, and end grain soaks up water like a bundle of straws. Porch rails and balusters are exposed on every side. Siding, by contrast, is mostly vertical and overlapped to shed water.

Sun matters as much as water. The south and west sides of a house take the most ultraviolet light and the biggest daily temperature swings, which break down paint film and open joints. It is common to find the trim on the north side in good shape while the south-facing sills are peeling.

In Saratoga Springs and across the Capital Region, freeze-thaw cycles make every small crack worse. Water that gets into a hairline gap in a sill or joint freezes, expands, and pushes the paint off the wood from behind.

## Signs your trim is due for paint

Walk around the house on a sunny day and look closely at the trim, starting with the south and west sides.

- **Cracking or "alligatoring."** Fine cracks in the paint surface let water in.
- **Peeling or flaking,** especially at the bottom of casings, along sills and at joints.
- **Chalking.** Rub the paint with a dark cloth. Heavy white residue means the paint is breaking down.
- **Open joints.** Gaps where casings meet sills or where boards butt together, often with failed caulk.
- **Bare or gray wood.** Any exposed wood should be primed and painted before winter.
- **Soft spots.** Press a screwdriver into sills and the bottom ends of casings and corner boards. If it sinks in, there is rot to deal with before paint.

If you find soft wood, our guide to [window sill rot repair](/blog/window-sill-rot-repair/) covers when sills can be repaired and when they need replacing.

## What shortens the interval

When paint fails in two or three years instead of seven, the cause is usually one of these:

- **Water from behind.** Overflowing gutters, missing drip edge, or damp walls push moisture through the wood and lift the paint. Our post on [soffit and fascia repair](/blog/gutters-soffits-fascia-rot/) covers the gutter side of this.
- **Poor preparation.** Paint over loose paint, dirt or mildew fails with the layer underneath.
- **Incompatible paints.** Latex paint applied directly over old oil-based paint without the right primer often peels in sheets.
- **Too many layers.** On old houses, a century of paint can build up so thick that it cracks and lets go under its own stiffness. Eventually, the old paint has to come off to bare wood in those areas.
- **Bad weather during painting.** Paint applied when it is too cold, too humid, or just before rain does not cure properly. Follow the minimum temperature on the label, including overnight.

## A typical repaint schedule

| Trim location | Typical interval | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Window sills | 3 to 5 years | Horizontal and sun-exposed; check every year |
| Porch rails and balusters | 3 to 5 years | Exposed on all sides; see [porch railing repair](/blog/porch-railing-inspection/) |
| South and west casings and corner boards | 5 to 7 years | Sun breaks paint down faster |
| North and east trim | 7 to 10 years | Watch for mildew instead of sun damage |
| Fascia and soffit | 5 to 10 years | Shorter if gutters overflow |

## Doing it right on an older house

A lasting trim paint job is mostly preparation. A sound sequence on an older home is to wash, scrape loose paint, sand edges smooth, repair or replace rotted wood, prime all bare wood, caulk joints that should be sealed, and finish with two coats of a quality exterior paint. Leave the bottom edges of clapboards and certain joints uncaulked where they are meant to let water drain out.

### Lead paint

If your house was built before 1978, assume the old trim paint may contain lead until it is tested. Scraping and sanding lead paint creates hazardous dust and chips, which is a particular danger to children and pregnant women. Test before disturbing it. Federal rules set specific practices for contractors working on lead paint in older homes, so ask anyone you hire how they will contain dust and clean up. The [EPA's lead renovation page](https://www.epa.gov/lead) explains the basics for homeowners.

### Saratoga Springs historic districts

Saratoga Springs has several historic districts, and exterior changes there can need Design Review Commission approval. Repainting in the same colors is often treated differently from changing materials, replacing trim with a different profile, or swapping wood for PVC. Check with the city before starting if you are in a district and planning more than a repaint.

### Repair or replace the wood

Rot in a short section of trim can often be cut out and patched with a matching piece of wood, or filled with an epoxy consolidant and filler. Replacement makes sense when rot runs the length of a board. For decorative trim such as brackets, scroll-cut balusters and moldings that are no longer made, matching the original profile keeps the character of the house. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can exterior trim be painted in the fall?

Yes, if the temperature during application and overnight stays above the minimum on the paint label, and the trim is dry. Some exterior paints are made for lower temperatures. In the Capital Region, early fall is the safer bet, because short days, dew and cold nights limit drying time by late October.

### Should I replace wood trim with PVC so I never have to paint?

PVC and composite trim do not rot and do not need paint to survive, though many owners paint them to match. On newer houses it is a practical choice. On older and historic houses, profile and appearance matter, and historic district rules may limit material changes. Sound original wood trim is usually worth keeping and maintaining.

### Is it better to scrape and paint or wait for a full repaint?

Spot repairs on sills and other worn areas are worth doing as soon as paint fails, because bare wood takes on water every time it rains. Waiting for a full repaint often means paying for wood repair that a small touch-up would have prevented.
