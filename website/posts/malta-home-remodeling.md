Most remodeling in Malta, NY starts with whatever the builder did on a budget and is now wearing out: the original pressure-treated deck, the builder-grade bathrooms, the thin trim and hollow doors, and a kitchen that works fine but doesn't fit how the family lives anymore. Malta's houses are mostly newer than the old villages around it, so the work looks different from old-house restoration. The framing is generally sound. What's worn out is the finish, along with the choices that got made in a hurry when the house went up.

## Why Malta houses wear out on a schedule

A lot of Malta was built out in subdivisions over the last few decades, with older farmhouses on the back roads and the historic houses in the village of Round Lake mixed in. In a subdivision, one builder often put up many houses from a handful of plans over a few years. That has a handy side effect. The houses on your street tend to need the same things at about the same time. If the deck two doors down got replaced last summer, yours may not be far behind.

Newer construction also means fewer surprises in the walls than a 120-year-old house in Saratoga Springs. You're unlikely to open a wall and find knob-and-tube wiring or balloon framing. What you'll find is the builder's standard package everywhere you look. One vanity style. Flat-panel doors. Skinny baseboard. A single bath fan that was never strong enough. Most Malta remodeling replaces those.

## The deck that came with the house

The original deck is often the first thing on a house this age that needs real money. Pressure-treated decks built when the subdivision went in have been through a lot of winters by now: snow sitting on them, freeze-thaw, wet leaves rotting in the gaps between boards.

Get under yours with a flashlight and look. Boards that are cupped, split or soft show up first near the house and under the downspouts. Push on the railings; they shouldn't move. Look for rust on the nails, screws and joist hangers. Check the ledger, the board bolted to the house, for staining or rot behind it. And walk the stairs. If they bounce or have shifted on their footings, that's a problem.

Our post on [signs your deck needs to be replaced](/blog/signs-your-deck-needs-replacing/) covers how to tell a deck that needs new boards from one that needs a new frame. If you're replacing it, take another look at materials while you're at it. We go through the tradeoffs in [composite vs wood decking in upstate NY](/blog/composite-vs-wood-decking-upstate-ny/).

The code has likely changed since your deck was built. The New York State Residential Code requires guards where a deck surface is more than 30 inches above grade, at least 36 inches high, with openings a 4-inch sphere can't pass through, and a handrail on stairs with four or more risers. A deck built years ago may not meet that, and a replacement has to. Your town building department can tell you what permit and inspections apply.

## Builder-grade bathrooms

Bathrooms in newer Malta houses tend to fail the same ways. The fiberglass tub surround has gone dull or cracked. The particleboard vanity has swollen up around the sink. The exhaust fan is too small, or it dumps into the attic instead of outside, and you can see it in the peeling paint and mildew on the ceiling. The caulk has failed and been redone more than once.

That doesn't always mean a full gut. If the walls are sound and the layout works, a bathroom can be updated in stages: new vanity and top, new light and fan, new floor, and a tiled shower later. Our guide to [updating a dated bathroom](/blog/dated-bathroom-update-ideas/) goes through which updates make the biggest difference and which ones belong together. One exception. If water has been getting behind the surround, the walls have to be opened up and dried before anything new goes on them.

## Kitchens that work but don't fit

Most Malta kitchens function fine. The complaints are about layout and storage. The island's too small or it's in the way. The upper cabinets stop a foot short of the ceiling. There's no counter next to the range, and the pantry is one narrow cabinet. A family that bought the house with toddlers finds the kitchen doesn't work for teenagers, company and two people cooking at once.

Start with the cabinet boxes. If they're solid and the layout's right, refacing or painting can be enough. If the layout is the problem, we'd put the money into new cabinets. Our post on whether to [reface or replace kitchen cabinets](/blog/kitchen-cabinets-reface-or-replace/) helps you sort that out before you talk to anyone about price.

## Trim, doors and built-ins

The quiet upgrade in a newer house is the millwork. Thin colonial baseboard, flat casing around the doors and windows, and hollow-core doors are standard in most subdivision houses. Swap them for taller baseboard, heavier casing and solid-core doors and the house feels different, more than you'd guess from the size of the job. It can go room by room, too. We cover it in [upgrading builder-grade trim and doors](/blog/upgrade-builder-grade-trim-and-doors/). A built-in bookcase, a mudroom bench or an office wall fits right into the same project.

## What to do first

With several projects on the list, do them in this order:

1. Anything letting water in or that isn't safe: a failing deck ledger, loose railings, a leaking shower.
2. The messy inside work, like the kitchen or the trim, grouped so you only live through the dust once.
3. Built-ins and other finish pieces last.

The building season is short here, so plan the deck and outside work over the winter for a spring or summer start. Inside projects can run right through the cold months.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are remodeling permits handled by Malta or by the county?

Building permits for most residential work in Malta go through the town's building department, not the county. Which projects need a permit can change, so confirm with the town before work starts. Your contractor should be able to tell you which parts of the job need a permit and inspections.

### Does it make sense to remodel a house that's only twenty or thirty years old?

Often, yes. The structure of a newer house is generally in good shape, so more of the budget goes into finishes and layout and less into repairs. Decks, bathrooms and trim are where a newer house shows its age first.

### Should I do several projects at once or one at a time?

Group the ones that share trades or tear up the same rooms; that saves time and mess. Work in a different part of the house, or outside, can be scheduled on its own without much penalty. A phased plan lets you do the urgent work now and the rest when the budget allows.
