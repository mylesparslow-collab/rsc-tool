Kitchen remodeling in Wilton, NY mostly means updating a kitchen in a house built in the last few decades, not rescuing a century-old room. A lot of Wilton's housing is in the subdivisions that grew up north of Saratoga Springs, and those kitchens look alike: builder-grade cabinets, a soffit boxed in over the wall cabinets, a small island, and the kitchen open to a family room. The bones of the house are generally fine. The work is in the layout, the storage, the lighting, and picking materials that can take a busy household.

## The kitchen most Wilton houses came with

No two houses are the same, but walk into enough kitchens in Wilton's newer neighborhoods and you see the pattern. Oak, maple or thermofoil cabinets in stock sizes, often with a soffit above. Laminate or early granite counters with a tile or 4-inch backsplash. An island or peninsula that's too small to seat the family or so close to the counters you can't work around it. A microwave over the range that blows the smoke back in your face where a vented hood ought to be. One ceiling fixture or a handful of recessed cans, and not much light on the counters. Vinyl or wood-look flooring that runs out into the family room and down the hall.

The farmhouses and older homes on Wilton's back roads are another story. Those kitchens may have been redone several times, and they come with old-house conditions: floors that aren't level, older wiring, and in a house built before 1978, possible lead paint, which should be tested before demolition.

## Keep what's good

In a newer house you don't have to tear everything out. Go through the room and sort it into what stays, what changes, and what gets replaced. That alone saves time and money.

The room's footprint, the window positions and the subfloor usually stay. Sometimes the cabinet boxes do too. If they're solid and the layout works, our article on [refacing or replacing kitchen cabinets](/blog/kitchen-cabinets-reface-or-replace/) walks through how to decide.

The island, the range hood, the lighting, the pantry and the backsplash are the things people most often change.

Worn laminate counters, tired drawer slides, and thermofoil doors peeling near the oven or dishwasher get replaced. So does dated flooring, if the cabinets are coming out anyway.

## Layout fixes for an open plan

The open plans in a lot of Wilton homes give you more to work with than a cut-up old house does. A few changes come up again and again.

### A bigger island, or a better-placed one

A bigger island adds seats and storage, but it needs clear walkways all the way around. A cramped aisle between the island and the range or sink is more aggravating than no island at all. If you want a sink or cooktop in the island, the plumbing or venting has to come up through the floor. Over an unfinished basement that's simple. Over a slab or a finished ceiling it's a bigger job.

### Taking out the soffits

With the soffits gone, the wall cabinets can run to the ceiling. You get more storage and the room looks cleaner. But soffits sometimes hide ductwork, plumbing vents or wiring, so we open up a section and look inside before the design is final.

### A real pantry

A walk-in or tall cabinet pantry is a common wish in a family house. Sometimes the space comes from a nearby coat closet or a stretch of mudroom wall.

### Venting the range outside

Swapping the over-the-range microwave for a hood that vents outdoors clears cooking smoke and moisture out of the house. Where that duct can go, out a side wall or up through the roof, depends on the framing overhead. If the second floor is built on engineered I-joists or the roof on trusses, you can't just cut through them wherever you like.

## Materials that hold up to a family

In a kitchen that feeds a family every day, pick for durability over fashion. Quartz counters don't need sealing and shrug off most stains. Plywood cabinet boxes survive a leak under the sink better than particleboard. Full-extension drawers in the base cabinets beat doors with shelves behind them, because you can see what's in the back. Luxury vinyl plank and porcelain tile handle snow, salt and wet boots coming in from the garage better than some wood floors.

Lighting is one of the most noticeable upgrades you can make in a newer kitchen. Put under-cabinet lights over the counters, pendants over the island, and recessed lights on a dimmer, so each part of the room gets its own light. New circuits should be installed by a licensed electrician, and countertop outlets need ground-fault protection.

If the rest of the house still has builder-grade trim and hollow doors, the kitchen job is a natural time to plan for those as well. Our post on [upgrading builder-grade trim and doors](/blog/upgrade-builder-grade-trim-and-doors/) covers that work.

## Permits, the HOA, and the order of work

Adding circuits, moving plumbing, changing walls or cutting in a new exterior vent generally needs a building permit. Check with the Town of Wilton building department before work starts, since requirements and procedures change. Some neighborhoods have homeowner association rules about exterior changes like vents or windows, so check those too.

If you're on a private well, the remodel is a sensible time to think about water treatment equipment under the sink or in the basement, since the plumbing is already open.

The sequence doesn't vary much. Design and ordering come first, then demolition, then rough plumbing and electrical with inspections. After that comes wall repair, flooring if it's being replaced, and cabinets. The countertops get templated, made and installed, and then the backsplash, fixtures and appliances go in. Pick everything before demolition and the kitchen is out of service for less time.

## What moves the price

In a Wilton kitchen, the big cost drivers are whether the cabinets get replaced or refaced, the countertop material, any change to the island or the plumbing, a new vented hood, and the lighting. Flooring is the sneaky one. In an open plan it often runs straight into the family room and hall, so changing it in the kitchen means changing it everywhere it connects. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit to remodel a kitchen in Wilton?

Generally yes if you're adding circuits, moving plumbing, changing walls or adding an exterior vent. Replacing counters or cabinets in the same spots may not need one. Ask the Town of Wilton building department to be sure.

### Should I take out the soffits above my cabinets?

Most of the time, yes. You gain storage and the ceiling reads taller. Some soffits hide ducts or pipes that have to be rerouted, which adds work, so we check inside before the design is settled.

### Should I replace the flooring when I remodel the kitchen?

If the cabinets are being replaced, that's the time. In an open-plan Wilton house the kitchen floor usually runs into other rooms, so decide early where the new flooring will stop.
